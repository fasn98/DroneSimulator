## Technical Specification Document for Exploration Drone Simulation Application

### 1. Introduction and Scope

#### 1.1 Primary Objective
The primary objective of this Python application is to simulate and model the behavior of exploration drones across a variety of environments and mission profiles. This includes the accurate representation of flight dynamics, interaction with different atmospheres and gravitational fields, and evaluation of drone performance under specific conditions.

#### 1.2 Target Audience and Applications
### The simulator is designed for:

| *   **Development Teams:** To provide an environment for testing and validating control algorithms, optimizing drone designs, and prototyping new functionalities. |
|---|


*   **Researchers:** To facilitate studies on aerodynamics in thin atmospheres, autonomous navigation in unknown environments, and environmental impact in space missions.
*   **Mission Planners:** To enable the validation of flight plans, risk assessment, and optimization of exploration strategies before real hardware implementation.

Potential applications include the design and testing of Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) for planetary exploration, mission feasibility analysis, and operator training.

#### 1.3 High-Level Capabilities
### The application shall offer the following high-level capabilities:

| *   Advanced physics simulation for drone models, including 6-Degrees-of-Freedom (6-DoF) dynamics. |
|---|


*   Modeling of varied environments, such as Earth's atmosphere, the thin Martian atmosphere, and the lunar vacuum, with specific gravity and conditions.
*   Management and execution of custom mission profiles for different objectives (reconnaissance, sample transportation, monitoring).
*   Comprehensive data output for telemetry and post-simulation analysis.
*   Basic visualization functionalities and a Command-Line Interface (CLI).

### 2. Functional Requirements

#### 2.1 Drone Model Configuration
The simulator must be able to configure and load various drone models with their detailed characteristics.
*   **Data Input:** The application needs the ability to input and parse detailed drone characteristics, such as dimensions, mass, Maximum Take-Off Weight (MTOW), propulsion system specifications (thrust curves, power consumption, efficiency), aerodynamic coefficients, control surface parameters, and sensor payload details. As per *Chapter Three* of your TCC, an example drone for light atmospheric exploration (e.g., Mars) has a **Total Height of 0.8 meters**, **Central Fuselage Length of 1.2 meters**, **Wingspan of 2.5 meters** (for quadrotor/hexarotor configuration), and **Rotor/Propeller Diameter of 0.6 meters per propeller**.
*   **Data Format:** Drone data must be provided from a structured data source (e.g., JSON, YAML, or a conceptual 'PDF equivalent'). This allows flexibility in defining parameters.
*   **Mass and Propulsion:** The system should incorporate an **Estimated Structural Weight (Operational Empty Weight - OEW) of 35 kg**. For **MTOW**, **70 kg for atmospheric operation (Mars)** and **100 kg for vacuum operation (Moon, with thrusters)** are considered. Mass distribution must be modeled to keep the Center of Gravity (CG) within strict operational limits, a crucial aspect for stability and control, as highlighted in the TCC.

#### 2.2 Physics-Based Simulation Engine
The core of the application will be a simulation engine that accurately models flight physics.
*   **Flight Dynamics Model:** Implement a 6-Degrees-of-Freedom (6-DoF) flight dynamics model, which is essential for simulating complex movements (translation and rotation in three axes).
*   **Gravitational Forces:** Accurate modeling of gravitational forces based on the selected environment (e.g., lower Martian gravity, even lower lunar gravity).
*   **Aerodynamic Calculations:** Precise calculations of aerodynamic forces and moments (lift, drag, side force, pitch, roll, and yaw moments) based on drone geometry, velocity, and atmospheric conditions. For Mars, this involves aerodynamic profiles optimized for low Reynolds numbers and low air density.
*   **Propulsion Modeling:** Detailed propulsion system modeling, including thrust generation (e.g., **15-20 N per motor** for Martian conditions), power/fuel consumption, and efficiency over time. The TCC mentions Brushless DC (BLDC) electric motors for atmospheric environments and cold gas thrusters or monopropellants (such as Hydrazine or "green" alternatives) for vacuum.
*   **Simulation Capability:** Support for real-time or accelerated time-step simulation, allowing efficient testing of long scenarios. The impact of MTOW on required thrust, energy/propellant consumption, and consequently, autonomy and flight time must be reflected, such as the **10-15 minutes** estimated for flight on Mars with maximum payload.

#### 2.3 Environmental Simulation
The simulator must support distinct, physically accurate environmental models, based on the challenges described in the TCC.
*   **Earth-based Atmosphere:** Incorporating variable atmospheric density, pressure, and temperature profiles with altitude; standard Earth gravity; and configurable wind models (e.g., constant, gust, shear).
*   **Martian Atmosphere:** Accounting for the thin CO2 atmosphere with specific density/pressure profiles; lower Martian gravity; and considerations for dust effects if applicable. *Chapter Three* emphasizes that, although tenuous (about 1% of Earth's), the Martian atmosphere allows the use of rotors for lift.
*   **Lunar Environment (Vacuum):** Simulating vacuum conditions (negligible atmosphere) and significantly lower lunar gravity. The TCC explains that the absence of atmosphere prevents aerodynamic propulsion, requiring the use of Reaction Control Systems (RCS) with thrusters.
*   **Initial Configurations:** Allow configurable start locations, velocities, and orientations for the drone within each environment.

#### 2.4 Mission Profile Management
The application must allow the definition and execution of various missions.
*   **Definition & Loading:** Implement the ability to define, load, and execute various mission profiles, each with specific objectives and customizable parameters.
*   **Mission Types:**
    *   **Fast Area Recognition & Mapping:** Define success metrics based on area covered per unit time and sensor effectiveness. Include basic sensor simulation (e.g., field of view, range). The TCC suggests a light quadrotor or hexarotor configuration for this mission on Mars, with a **wingspan of 2.0m** and **MTOW of 50kg**, with an autonomy of **20-25 minutes**.
    *   **Samples Transportation:** Model payload handling (weight, dimensions), precise navigation requirements for pickup/delivery points, and simulated payload interaction mechanisms. For the Moon, the TCC proposes a robust structure with multiple monopropellant thrusters, with **dimensions of 1m x 1m x 0.8m** and **MTOW of 80kg**.
    *   **Extensive Area Monitoring:** Emphasize endurance, energy management, and efficient coverage patterns (e.g., lawnmower, spiral) for prolonged operations. The TCC mentions hybrid drones (VTOL with wings for efficient flight) or multirotors with efficient solar recharging, with a **wing span of 3-4m (for hybrid)** and **MTOW of 60kg**, aiming for **multiple hours of gliding flight (with solar recharge)**.
*   **Execution & Monitoring:** Provide mechanisms to track real-time mission progress, objective completion status, and key drone operational parameters (e.g., remaining fuel/battery, altitude, speed, payload status).

#### 2.5 Data Input & Output
The application must manage parameter input and simulation data logging.
*   **Input Format:** Specify the required structured format for inputting drone characteristics, environmental parameters, and mission profile definitions.
*   **Telemetry Logging:** Define the output telemetry data format, including timestamped values for position (x,y,z), velocity (vx,vy,vz), acceleration, attitude (roll, pitch, yaw), control inputs, thrust, fuel/power consumption, simulated sensor readings, and environmental conditions. The TCC highlights the importance of a Battery Management System (BMS) for real-time monitoring of voltage, current, temperature, and State of Health (SoH) of the battery.
*   **Persistent Logging:** Implement persistent logging of all simulation telemetry, ideally in easily parsable formats (e.g., CSV, JSON) for post-analysis.

#### 2.6 Basic Visualization & User Interface
A simple but effective interface is required to interact with the simulator.
*   **Command-Line Interface (CLI):** Provide a CLI for configuring simulations, initiating runs, and displaying basic real-time status.
*   **Plotting Capabilities:** Include requirements for basic data plotting capabilities (e.g., using Matplotlib) for visualizing drone trajectory (2D/3D), altitude profiles, speed over time, and mission progress.

### 3. Non-Functional Requirements

#### 3.1 Performance
*   **Computational Efficiency:** The simulation engine should be computationally efficient, aiming for faster-than-real-time execution for typical mission scenarios, allowing rapid design and test iterations.

#### 3.2 Modularity
*   **Extensible Architecture:** The application architecture must be modular and extensible, allowing easy addition of new drone models, environments, sensors, and mission types without significant code refactoring. The TCC emphasizes "modular design" to facilitate assembly and testing, and for potential replacements by robots in future space infrastructures.

#### 3.3 Accuracy
*   **Validated Physics Models:** Physics models should be based on established scientific principles and validated for reasonable accuracy against theoretical predictions or benchmark cases, ensuring the fidelity of the simulation.

#### 3.4 Scalability
*   **Concurrent Simulation:** Consider the potential for simulating multiple drones concurrently in future iterations, which would be valuable for cooperative missions or swarm scenarios.

#### 3.5 Usability
*   **Clear Documentation:** Ensure clear documentation for configuration file formats and CLI commands to facilitate user interaction and reduce the learning curve.

### 6. Testing and Validation Plan

#### 6.1 Unit Testing
*   **Strategies:** Describe strategies for unit testing individual components, such as physics calculations (e.g., gravity, drag, thrust) and drone model parameter parsing. This ensures that each part of the code functions as expected.
